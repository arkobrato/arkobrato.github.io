Long-term abstinence from opioids is associated with persistent changes in brain structure that may underlie continued relapse vulnerability, particularly in the nucleus accumbens, a brain region central to reward and drug-seeking behavior. This preprint examines dendritic spine density (a structural marker of synaptic connectivity) in the nucleus accumbens following protracted abstinence, specifically in rats previously classified as vulnerable to heroin use disorder, to characterize how abstinence-related neural changes differ by vulnerability phenotype.

*I contributed to this collaborative, multi-site study as part of the Kalivas/Kuhn lab heroin vulnerability research program.*
