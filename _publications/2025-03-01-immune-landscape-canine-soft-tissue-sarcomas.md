Soft tissue sarcomas (STS) occur naturally in both humans and dogs, and dogs have been proposed as a comparative oncology model because their tumors arise spontaneously, share environmental exposures with their owners, and often resemble human disease histologically. However, the immune landscape of canine STS, which is increasingly recognized as important for treatment response in human sarcomas, has been less well characterized.

This study analyzed clinical, histopathologic, and gene expression data from 75 dogs with soft tissue sarcoma to characterize their immune microenvironment. We found that higher infiltration of FOXP3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs), an immunosuppressive cell population, was associated with shorter progression-free intervals, suggesting that Treg-driven immune suppression may contribute to disease progression in canine STS, similar to findings reported in several human cancers.

Histologically and transcriptomically, the tumors in this cohort most closely resembled human undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, the most common STS subtype in adults. Together, these findings support canine soft tissue sarcoma as a relevant and underused preclinical model for studying the tumor immune microenvironment and testing immunotherapy approaches relevant to human sarcoma patients.
